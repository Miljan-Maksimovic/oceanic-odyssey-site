The day at Phi Phi

The crossing is part of the day: a flybridge on Meraki, Pandora and Pearl, cabins below on most yachts.

  • Maya Bay. The cove from The Beach. The yacht stops in front of it for the view; a landing would take too much of the day.
  • Pileh Lagoon. A narrow gap in the cliffs, then calm, shallow water to swim in. Each yacht carries a small tender that goes in when the sea and the time allow.
  • Monkey Beach. Some days this is the swim stop.
  • The Viking Cave. 170 metres long, with swiftlets nesting on its ledges.
  • Bamboo Island and Koh Yung. White sand ringed by coral reef.
  • Phi Phi Don. The only inhabited island, with no private cars.

Lunch is in the rate on most of the yachts, and Amy takes special requests for Thai food on board. Snorkel gear is in every rate.

Park fees

  • Park fee. Park fees are paid by each guest, in cash, at the park. Your captain or hostess helps with the local formalities. Only Song of Songs lists them in its rate.
  • E-ticket. A face-scan e-ticket was introduced at Maya Bay, Bamboo Island and Koh Poda from 15 October 2025. Tickets are bought in advance or before 08:00 on the day, and foreign guests are identified by passport, so bring passports.

What to expect at Phi Phi

Bring swimwear, sunglasses, a change of clothes and your passport. Towels are on board. Sunscreen containing oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-methylbenzylidene camphor or butylparaben is banned in Thailand's marine national parks, so choose a reef-safe one.

What is the best boat from Phuket to Phi Phi?

To reach Phi Phi Don alone, the public ferry takes about two hours. For the islands themselves, a private yacht gives your group the whole boat and a crew.

When to go

Around Phi Phi the water is clearest, with the smallest waves, from about November or December into May. Maya Bay closes for August and September.

    Can you swim at Maya Bay?

    No, the park does not allow it. The yacht stops in front of the bay for the view, and the swimming is at Pileh Lagoon, at Monkey Beach and over the reefs.
