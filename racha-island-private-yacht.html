South of Phuket, Racha Yai and Racha Noi sit in clear blue water: powdery white sand at Patok Bay, reef a short swim from the beach, and deeper sites off Racha Noi. A paradise for scuba divers, and just as good for free diving and snorkelling.

Racha Yai's beaches

Patok Bay, a crescent of soft white sand shelving gently into clear water, and Siam Bay on the north coast, longer and quieter.

In the water off Racha Yai

The reef starts in the shallows a short swim from the beach, with 15 to 25 metres of visibility in season, and many of Phuket's new divers make their first dive here.

Racha Noi, about 6 km further south, is uninhabited and rocky, with the deeper sites certified divers come for; manta rays pass between January and March. Scuba, free diving or snorkelling: tell us which, and we plan the day around it.

When to go

November to April is the season for Racha, when the Andaman Sea is more likely to be calm and the water clear. From about mid-May to mid-October the south-west monsoon brings cloud and rain, and the west coast of Racha Yai can turn rough, so boats land on the east side instead, where the water is greener.

Questions

Is Racha Island worth visiting?

Yes, if the day is about the water. Racha Yai is known for clear water and white sand, the reef sits within a short swim of its beaches, and its shallow dive sites are where many divers in Phuket start. The island charges no admission.
