The day at Racha

Racha Yai, the larger of the two islands, has the beaches. Racha Noi lies about 6 km further south, uninhabited and rocky, with no real beach.

  • Patok Bay. A crescent of soft white sand that shelves gently into clear water, with the reef starting close to the beach. In season, visibility runs 15 to 25 metres.
  • Siam Bay. The longest beach on Racha Yai, on the north coast, and quieter than Patok because fewer day boats stop there.
  • A dive. Racha Yai is where many of Phuket's new divers make their first descent: shallow sites and mild currents. Certified divers go deeper off Racha Noi, where manta rays pass between January and March. A scuba dive at Racha can be arranged as part of the charter.
  • Coral Island. Koh Hae in Thai, off Phuket's south coast. Long Beach is the quieter of its two beaches: white sand under tall shade trees and shallow turquoise water. Make Coral the last stop of the afternoon, when Long Beach is at its quietest.

Lunch is in the rate on most of the yachts, and Amy takes special requests for Thai food on board. Snorkel gear is in every rate. On Meraki, the wakeboard, the kneeboard and two towable tubes come out between swims.

Fees and rules at Racha

  • No park fee. Neither island is in a national park, and there is no admission charge.
  • Protected reefs. The beaches, coral and reef life of both islands are protected, and mooring buoys are in place. Breaking the protection rules carries a fine.
  • Landing. Boats land at Patok Bay from November to April. From May to October, when the west coast can be too rough, they land at Ter Bay, on the sheltered east side.

When to go

November to April is the season for Racha, when the Andaman Sea is more likely to be calm and the water clear. From about mid-May to mid-October the south-west monsoon brings cloud and rain, and the west coast of Racha Yai can turn rough, so boats land on the east side instead, where the water is greener.

    Questions

    Is Racha Island worth visiting?

    Yes, if the day is about the water. Racha Yai is known for clear water and white sand, the reef sits within a short swim of its beaches, and its shallow dive sites are where many divers in Phuket start. The island charges no admission.
